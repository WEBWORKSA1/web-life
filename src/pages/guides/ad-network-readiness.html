Getting ad-network ready

Ad networks approve sites that are useful, transparent and easy to navigate. This checklist covers what reviewers look for, and the mistakes that get accounts limited later.

By Web.Life EditorialUpdated 26 November 20264 min read

What reviewers are looking for

Ad networks want to place their advertisers' ads next to content people value, on sites that treat visitors fairly. Their reviews look at three things: the content (original, substantial, allowed by policy), the site (navigable, complete, transparent about who runs it) and the behaviour (no tricks that inflate clicks or views). Get those right and approval is mostly a matter of time.

Content

  • Original and useful. Write pages that add something: your experience, data, examples or tools. Copied, spun or auto-generated pages with little added value are a common reason for rejection ("low-value content").
  • Enough of it. There's no official page count, but a site with a handful of thin pages looks unfinished. Publish a solid set of complete pages across your topic before applying.
  • Policy-compliant. Read the network's content policies. Some topics are prohibited outright; others are restricted and earn less.
  • No under-construction pages. Remove placeholders, "coming soon" sections and broken links.

Required pages and transparency

  • About: who runs the site and why.
  • Contact: a working way to reach you.
  • Privacy policy: it must explain that third parties, including Google, use cookies to serve ads based on previous visits, and how visitors can opt out of personalised advertising.
  • Terms of use, and disclosures for affiliate links and sponsored content.
  • Clear navigation: a visitor should reach every important page within a couple of clicks.

Technical setup

  • Your own domain with HTTPS, and you must be able to edit the site's HTML to add the ad code to every page.
  • ads.txt: a plain text file at your domain's root that lists who is authorised to sell your ad space. For AdSense it contains a line with your publisher ID. Missing or wrong ads.txt can reduce earnings.
  • Consent for European visitors. Google requires publishers serving ads to users in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland to use a Google-certified consent management platform. AdSense offers its own consent message you can switch on in the Privacy & messaging section.
  • Mobile-friendly, reasonably fast pages. Reviewers and visitors both notice.

Layout rules that protect your account

  • Never ask, encourage or reward anyone for clicking ads, including friends and family.
  • Never click your own ads, even to test them. Use the network's preview tools instead.
  • Don't place ads where they can be mistaken for navigation, download buttons or content.
  • Don't let ads cover content or push it around while people read (it also hurts your CLS score).
  • Keep ads off pages without real content: error pages, thank-you pages, login screens.

Traffic quality

Buy traffic only from reputable sources, and never from schemes that promise thousands of visitors for a few dollars. Bot traffic and incentivised visits are treated as invalid activity and can lead to withheld earnings or account closure. Organic search, newsletters, social sharing and genuine referrals are what networks want to see.

After approval

  1. Start with automatic ad placement, then adjust density if pages feel cluttered.
  2. Watch your Core Web Vitals after enabling ads; reserve space for ad slots.
  3. Check the policy centre in your ad account monthly.
  4. Keep publishing. RPMs rise as your content and audience mature.
