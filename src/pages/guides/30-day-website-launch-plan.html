The 30-day website launch plan

Thirty days is enough to launch a website properly if you do things in the right order. This plan front-loads the decisions that are hard to undo and leaves polish for the end.

By Web.Life EditorialUpdated 26 November 20264 min read

Before day one: write the sentence

Every successful site can be summed up in one sentence: "[Site] helps [who] to [do what], so they can [result]." Write yours before you buy anything. If you can't, the site will drift, and drifting sites rarely get traffic. Keep the sentence somewhere visible; you'll use it to decide what goes on the homepage, what gets cut, and how you describe the site to search engines.

Week 1 (days 1–7): foundations

The first week is about decisions that are expensive to change later.

  • Day 1, the name. Shortlist five names, say them aloud to two people, and check trademarks and social handles. Our domain guide covers the full checklist.
  • Day 2, the registration. Register the domain for at least two years in an account with two-factor authentication. Turn on auto-renew and registrar lock.
  • Day 3, the platform. Choose a website builder, a content management system or a static site based on who will update it. See builder, CMS or static.
  • Day 4, the budget. Price the first year with the launch cost calculator, using renewal prices rather than introductory ones.
  • Days 5–6, the structure. List every page you need for launch. For most sites that's home, about, contact, a privacy policy, terms, and three to five pages of core content. Sketch the navigation on paper.
  • Day 7, email. Set up an address on your domain and the SPF, DKIM and DMARC records your email provider gives you, so your messages don't land in spam.

Week 2 (days 8–14): content first

Write before you design. Real words change layouts, and placeholder text hides problems until it's too late.

  • Days 8–9, the homepage. In one screen, say who the site is for, what they can do here, and what to click next. Then add proof: examples, credentials or results.
  • Day 10, about and contact. Real names, a real story, a working way to get in touch. These pages build trust with visitors and with ad networks.
  • Days 11–14, core pages. Write three to five genuinely useful pages that answer the questions your audience actually asks. Each page should have one clear purpose and one call to action.

Rule of thumb: if a page would be just as useful on a competitor's site with the logo swapped, it isn't finished. Add your experience, your numbers or your examples.

Week 3 (days 15–21): build and connect

  • Days 15–17, design. Apply a simple, accessible design. Check colour contrast with the palette generator and test every page on a real phone.
  • Day 18, forms. Test the contact form from a different email address. Confirm messages arrive and replies work.
  • Day 19, legal pages. Publish a privacy policy that matches what you actually collect, plus terms of use. If you'll show ads or affiliate links, say so.
  • Day 20, analytics. Install one analytics tool and confirm it records visits. Exclude your own visits if you can.
  • Day 21, speed. Run PageSpeed Insights on the homepage and the heaviest page. Compress images and remove anything you don't need.

Week 4 (days 22–30): launch checks and go-live

  • Day 22, HTTPS and redirects. Make sure every version of your address (with and without www, http and https) ends up at one secure URL.
  • Day 23, titles and descriptions. Give every page a unique title and meta description. Preview them with the SEO meta checker.
  • Day 24, sitemap and robots. Generate a sitemap and check robots.txt isn't blocking anything important.
  • Day 25, backups and security. Turn on automatic off-site backups and two-factor authentication everywhere. Do one test restore.
  • Day 26, monitoring. Add an uptime monitor that alerts your phone.
  • Day 27, the health check. Score the site with the 30-point health check and fix every red item you can.
  • Day 28, go live. Remove any "coming soon" or noindex settings. Verify the site in Google Search Console and submit the sitemap.
  • Days 29–30, tell people. Share the site with the people it's for: an email list, a community you're part of, partners and customers. Ask three of them what confused them, and fix it.

After day 30

A launch is a starting line. In the first three months, publish one useful page a week, check Search Console every Monday for indexing problems, and rerun the health check at the end of each month. Most sites that fail don't fail at launch; they fail in month three, when updates stop. Put the next four publishing dates in your calendar before you close this page.
