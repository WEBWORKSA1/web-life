The website glossary.

70 terms you'll meet while launching, growing, earning from, protecting and passing on a website, each in one or two plain sentences. The dot shows the stage it belongs to.

3-2-1 rule
Keep three copies of data, on two types of storage, with one copy off-site.
A record
A DNS record that points a domain to an IPv4 server address.
ads.txt
A text file listing the companies authorised to sell ads on your site, used to fight ad fraud.
Affiliate link
A tracked link that earns you a commission when a visitor buys from a partner.
Anchor text
The clickable words in a link. Descriptive anchor text helps people and search engines.
Backlink
A link from another website to yours. Relevant, trusted backlinks are a strong ranking signal.
Backup
A copy of your site's files, database and settings that you can restore after a problem.
Bounce rate
The share of visits that leave without further interaction. Context matters; a quick answer can be a good visit.
Canonical URL
A tag that tells search engines which version of a page is the main one when similar versions exist.
CDN
Content delivery network: servers around the world that cache your files closer to visitors for faster loading.
CLS
Cumulative Layout Shift: how much content unexpectedly moves. Good is 0.1 or less.
CMS
A content management system: software such as WordPress that lets you edit pages through an admin interface instead of code.
CNAME record
A DNS record that points one hostname to another, often used for www or for services like site builders.
Conversion rate
The share of visitors who complete a goal, such as a purchase, signup or enquiry.
Core Web Vitals
Three user-experience metrics: Largest Contentful Paint, Interaction to Next Paint and Cumulative Layout Shift.
CPC
Cost per click: what an advertiser pays when someone clicks an ad.
CPM
Cost per thousand impressions: what an advertiser pays for one thousand ad views.
Crawling
The process of search engine bots discovering pages by following links and sitemaps.
CTR
Click-through rate: clicks divided by impressions, as a percentage.
Data breach
An incident in which information is exposed to people who shouldn't have it.
DDoS
Distributed denial of service: flooding a site with traffic to knock it offline.
Digital legacy
What happens to your online accounts, websites and data when you can no longer manage them.
DNS
The Domain Name System, the internet's directory that translates domain names into the numeric addresses of servers.
Domain name
The human-readable address of a website, such as web.life. You register the right to use it for a set term through a registrar.
Due diligence
A buyer's checks of a site's traffic, revenue, costs and ownership before purchase.
Earnout
Part of a sale price paid later, depending on how the business performs after the sale.
Escrow
A neutral service that holds the buyer's payment until assets are transferred to them.
Favicon
The small icon that represents your site in browser tabs and bookmarks.
Funnel
The steps a visitor takes from first visit to becoming a lead or customer.
Handover pack
A document listing every account, process and credential location a new owner needs.
HTTPS
The secure version of HTTP. Browsers warn visitors about pages that aren't served over it.
Indexing
When a search engine stores and understands a page so it can appear in results.
INP
Interaction to Next Paint: how quickly a page responds to input. Good is 200 milliseconds or less.
Internal link
A link from one page on your site to another, helping visitors and search engines navigate.
Invalid traffic
Clicks or views that don't come from genuine interest, such as bots or incentivised clicks. Ad networks don't pay for it.
LCP
Largest Contentful Paint: how long until the main content is visible. Good is 2.5 seconds or less.
Lead generation
Capturing enquiries from people who want a product or service, for your own business or a paying partner.
Legacy contact
A person you nominate on a platform to access or manage your account after you die or become inactive.
Malware
Malicious software, such as code injected into a hacked website to redirect visitors or steal data.
Meta description
A short summary in a page's HTML that search engines may show under the title in results.
Nameservers
The servers that hold your domain's DNS records. Changing them moves control of your DNS to another provider.
Net profit
Revenue minus all costs of running the site. The basis of most website valuations.
Noindex
A directive that asks search engines not to show a page in results.
Password manager
An app that generates, stores and fills unique passwords for every account.
Phishing
Fake messages designed to trick you into revealing passwords or installing malware.
Redemption period
A window after a domain expires during which the previous owner can still recover it, usually for a fee.
Registrar
A company accredited to register domain names on your behalf and manage their renewal and settings.
Registrar lock
A setting that blocks domain transfers until you remove it, preventing hijacking.
Responsive design
A layout that adapts to any screen size, from phones to wide monitors.
robots.txt
A file at your site's root that asks crawlers not to fetch certain paths.
RPM
Revenue per thousand pageviews (or impressions), the standard way to compare ad earnings.
Search Console
Google's free tool for monitoring how your site performs in Google Search and fixing indexing problems.
SEO
Search engine optimisation: improving how search engines find, understand and rank your pages.
SERP
Search engine results page: the page of results shown for a query.
SLA
Service level agreement: a provider's formal promise about availability or support, often with credits if missed.
Sponsored content
Content paid for by an advertiser. It must be clearly labelled.
SSL/TLS certificate
A file that lets browsers connect to your site over encrypted HTTPS and confirms the site's identity.
Staging site
A private copy of your website used to test changes before they go live.
Static site
A website made of pre-built files served as-is, with no database. Fast, cheap and secure to host.
Structured data
Machine-readable information, usually JSON-LD using schema.org vocabulary, that describes a page's content.
Title tag
The HTML element that sets a page's title, shown in browser tabs and usually as the headline in search results.
TLD (top-level domain)
The part of a domain after the last dot, like .com, .org or .life. Country-code TLDs such as .ca serve specific countries.
Two-factor authentication (2FA)
A second proof of identity, such as an app code or security key, required in addition to a password.
Uptime
The share of time a website is available. 99.9% allows about 44 minutes of downtime a month.
UTM parameters
Tags added to a link that tell analytics which source, medium and campaign sent a visit.
Valuation multiple
The number by which average monthly (or annual) profit is multiplied to estimate a website's sale price.
WAF
Web application firewall: a filter that blocks common attacks before they reach your site.
Web hosting
A service that stores your website's files and serves them to visitors over the internet.
Website builder
An all-in-one subscription service that bundles hosting, design tools and maintenance.
XML sitemap
A file listing the pages you want search engines to find, with optional update dates.
