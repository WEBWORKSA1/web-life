Legacy stage

Legacy: know what it's worth and who gets the keys.

Every website eventually changes hands: sold, merged, handed to a partner or inherited. Planning for that day makes the site more valuable today.

A website is an asset only if someone else can run it. Buyers pay for documented revenue, stable traffic and clean handover; families need to know which accounts exist and how to reach them. Both start with the same habit: write things down.

The legacy stage covers two paths:

  1. Valuation. Understand how buyers price sites (typically a multiple of average monthly profit) and what raises or lowers that multiple.
  2. Sale preparation. Clean profit-and-loss records, traffic history, documented processes and transferable accounts.
  3. Handover notes. One document listing your registrar, host, DNS, email, analytics and payment accounts.
  4. Trusted contacts. Use the legacy-contact features many platforms now offer, and tell someone where your instructions live.
  5. Renewals. Long registration terms and auto-renew so nothing lapses during a difficult time.
