Password or passphrase?

Both are strong when they're long and random. A random password packs the most strength into the fewest characters, which suits a password manager that types it for you. A passphrase of several randomly chosen words is easier to type and remember, which suits the few passwords you must enter by hand: your password manager, your computer and your main email.

What "bits of entropy" means

Entropy measures how many guesses an attacker needs. Each extra bit doubles the work. The estimate assumes the attacker knows exactly how the password was generated (the character set or the word list) and can test ten billion guesses per second offline, which is a pessimistic, safe assumption. Around 60 bits is reasonable for most online accounts protected by rate limits; aim for 80 or more for anything that guards other accounts.

Strength is only half of it

  • Use a different password for every account. Reuse is how one breach becomes ten.
  • Store them in a reputable password manager.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication wherever it's offered, starting with email, your domain registrar and your host.
  • Check whether your email appears in known breaches at Have I Been Pwned.
