    How the score works

    Each idea starts at 100 points and loses points for things that make a name harder to share: more than eight characters, hyphens, numbers, awkward clusters of consonants, tripled letters and an unusual balance of vowels. A .com earns a small bonus because people type it by default. The score is a quick filter, not a verdict. A slightly longer name that is instantly clear can beat a short one nobody can spell.

    The radio test

    Say each shortlisted name out loud to someone who hasn't seen it, then ask them to type it. If they hesitate over spelling, hyphens or "is that the number 4?", move on. Names that pass the radio test travel further through word of mouth.

    Before you buy

    1. Search the exact name and close variations to see who already uses it.
    2. Check your national trademark database and the WIPO Global Brand Database for conflicts in your field.
    3. Check that matching social handles are available, or close enough.
    4. Look at renewal pricing, not just the first-year price.
    5. Register in an account protected by two-factor authentication and switch on auto-renew.
