    How the estimate works

    Small websites are usually priced as a multiple of average monthly net profit. The calculator starts from a mid-range multiple and adjusts it for the factors buyers examine first: how long the site has existed, whether traffic is growing or shrinking, how many revenue streams it has, how dependent it is on a single traffic source, how much of the owner's time it needs, and whether it is documented well enough to hand over. The result is shown as a range because two buyers rarely see risk the same way.

    This is an indicative estimate for planning, not an appraisal or an offer. Actual prices depend on verified financials, the buyer's strategy, market conditions and the terms of the deal.

    What raises a multiple

    • Two or more years of steady or growing profit, with records that match the analytics.
    • Traffic from several sources rather than one search engine or one social network.
    • Revenue from several streams, ideally including some recurring income.
    • Low owner workload and written procedures.

    What lowers it

    • A recent traffic drop, especially after a search update.
    • Income that depends on the owner's personal brand or relationships.
    • Unclear ownership of content, images or accounts.
