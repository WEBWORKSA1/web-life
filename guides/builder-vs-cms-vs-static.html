Website builder, CMS or static site?

The best platform is the one you'll actually keep updated. Compare the three main approaches on the things that matter after launch day, not just how fast you can get started.

By Web.Life EditorialUpdated 26 November 20264 min read

The three approaches in one paragraph each

Website builders are all-in-one subscription services. Hosting, design tools, security updates and support come bundled. You edit visually, and the company runs the technology. The trade-off is that you work within their features, and moving to another platform later usually means rebuilding.

A content management system (CMS), most commonly WordPress, is software that runs on hosting you choose. It has a huge ecosystem of themes and plugins and can grow into almost anything. In exchange, you (or someone you pay) are responsible for updates, backups and security.

A static site is a set of pre-built HTML, CSS and JavaScript files served as-is. There's no database or server-side code to attack, so it is fast, cheap and very secure, and it can be hosted free on services such as GitHub Pages. Updating it usually means editing files or using a static site generator, so it suits people comfortable with a little technical work, or sites that change on a predictable schedule.

Five questions that decide it

  1. Who will update the site, and how often? If a non-technical person updates it weekly, a builder or a carefully set-up CMS wins. If it changes monthly and you're comfortable with files, static is hard to beat.
  2. What must it do? Brochure sites, portfolios, documentation and blogs work on all three. Stores, memberships and bookings are easier on a builder or a CMS with the right extensions.
  3. What's your monthly budget for software? Run the numbers with the launch cost calculator, including renewal prices and paid add-ons.
  4. How much does speed matter? For content sites that live on search traffic, speed affects both rankings and revenue. Static sites start fast; CMS sites need discipline.
  5. How likely are you to outgrow it? If you might need custom features in two years, pick something you can extend or export.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing by demo. Every platform looks great in its own showcase. Judge by what your site needs in year two.
  • Too many plugins. On a CMS, each plugin is more code to update and another possible vulnerability. Keep the list short and remove what you don't use.
  • Ignoring exit costs. Find out how to export your content before you write fifty pages into a platform.
  • No backup plan. Even managed platforms can lose content through account problems. Keep your own copies of important pages and images.

Our default recommendations

  • Local business or freelancer, non-technical owner: a reputable website builder.
  • Content site or blog aiming for search traffic and ad income: a lean CMS setup or a static site with a simple publishing workflow.
  • Online store: a dedicated e-commerce platform or a CMS with a mature store extension.
  • Developer, documentation, portfolio or tool site: static, hosted free or cheaply, with the source in version control.

Whatever you pick, the launch checklist is the same. Continue with the 30-day website launch plan.
