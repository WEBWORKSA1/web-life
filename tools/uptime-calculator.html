Reading an uptime promise

An uptime guarantee, or service level agreement (SLA), is usually measured per month. At 99.9% a host can be down for about 44 minutes a month and still keep its promise. Check three things in the small print: how downtime is measured (by the host or by you), what's excluded (scheduled maintenance often is), and what you get if they miss it (usually a partial credit, not compensation for lost sales).

Measure it yourself

An external uptime monitor checks your site every few minutes from outside your host and alerts you when it fails. Free plans are widely available. Point it at a page that exercises your database, not just a static file, so you catch the failures your visitors would actually see. Send alerts somewhere you'll notice at 3 a.m., like a phone notification, not a shared inbox.

Downtime is a business number

Multiply your average revenue per hour by the hours of downtime the SLA allows. If the result is more than the price difference to a better plan, the better plan pays for itself. For recovery planning, read backups, uptime and incident response.
