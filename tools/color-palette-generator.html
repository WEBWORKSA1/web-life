Choosing a harmony


    

  • Analogous colours sit next to each other on the colour wheel. Calm and cohesive; good for content sites.
    • 

  • Complementary pairs opposite hues. High energy; use one as the accent, sparingly.
    • 

  • Triadic spaces three hues evenly. Lively; balance it with plenty of neutral space.
    • 

  • Monochrome uses one hue at different lightness levels. Hard to get wrong.
    • 



Give every colour a job


A working website palette usually needs a background, a text colour, one accent for links and buttons, and one or two supporting colours for states like success and warning. Decide the job first, then pick the colour. Colours that carry meaning (an error red, a success green) should stay consistent across the whole site.


Contrast is not optional


Body text needs a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 against its background to meet WCAG AA; large text needs 3:1. Low-contrast grey text is the most common accessibility failure on the web and it makes pages harder to read for everyone, especially on phones outdoors. If a colour you love fails, use it for shapes and accents rather than text.
